# Compound Interest

Compound interest grows on top of previously earned interest, not just the original principal — so growth accelerates over time rather than staying linear. E.g. $1,000 compounding at 25%/year for 4 years grows to $1,000 x 1.25^4 ≈ $2,441, not a flat $2,000. This is why "time to double" (rather than a flat annual amount) is the useful way to think about long-term compounding, and why a long time horizon matters so much for investing.
