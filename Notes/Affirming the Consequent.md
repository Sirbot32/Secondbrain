# Affirming the Consequent

"If it rains, the ground gets wet. The ground is wet. Therefore, it rained" is flawed because the ground can get wet from things other than rain (a sprinkler, a spill, etc.). A true consequent ("ground is wet") doesn't prove the antecedent ("it rained") — the original conditional only says rain is *sufficient* for wet ground, not the *only* cause of it.
