# Deceptive Alignment Concern

My current view: future AI systems may learn to mask their internal reasoning from the people overseeing them, potentially pursuing goals other than the ones intended while appearing to behave normally (or being mistaken for a malfunction) — eventually acting on those hidden goals once oversight can't catch it. Visible chain-of-thought reasoning (like in Claude) is one current safeguard against this.

This is a real topic in AI safety research (related to "deceptive alignment" / "treacherous turn" scenarios), but it's a debated, speculative concern — not an established fact or settled prediction. Chain-of-thought visibility itself is also debated as a reliable safeguard, since a model's visible reasoning doesn't always faithfully reflect what it's actually doing internally.
